📚 Understanding DNS & Email Deliverability

What is a DNS Audit?

A DNS audit checks if your domain has the proper "email ID cards" that prove you're a legitimate sender. Without these, email providers like Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo treat your emails as suspicious — like someone trying to enter a building without an ID badge. The health score (0-100) shows how trustworthy your domain appears to spam filters.

SPF (Sender Policy Framework)

What it does: SPF is like a guest list for your domain. It tells email providers "These are the only servers allowed to send emails from my domain." If someone tries to send from an unlisted server, it's like crashing a party — email providers will reject it.

Example: v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all

💡 Fix if missing: Add a TXT record in your DNS settings with the SPF value from your email provider (Gmail, Outlook, etc.).

DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail)

What it does: DKIM adds a digital signature to every email you send — like a wax seal on an envelope. The recipient can verify this signature to prove the email really came from you and wasn't tampered with in transit.

Example: v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=MIGfMA0GCSqGSIb3DQEBAQUAA4...

💡 Fix if missing: Generate a DKIM key in your email provider's admin panel and add the TXT record they give you to your DNS.

DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication)

What it does: DMARC tells email providers what to do if SPF or DKIM fail. It's like telling security: "If someone's ID doesn't check out, quarantine them" or "Reject them completely." It also sends you reports about who's sending emails using your domain.

Example: v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; rua=mailto:dmarc@yourdomain.com

💡 Fix if missing: Add a DMARC TXT record at _dmarc.yourdomain.com. Start with p=none (monitoring), then upgrade to p=quarantine or p=reject once you're confident.

MX (Mail Exchange) Records

What it does: MX records tell the world where to deliver emails sent TO your domain. Without MX records, people can't reply to your emails — it's like having a return address but no mailbox.

💡 Fix if missing: Add MX records from your email provider to your DNS settings.

Reverse DNS (rDNS / PTR)

What it does: Reverse DNS proves your SMTP server's IP address matches its domain name. It's like checking that the address on someone's driver's license matches where they claim to live. Many spam filters reject emails from servers without rDNS.

💡 Fix if missing: Contact your hosting provider or ISP to set up a PTR record for your SMTP server's IP address. This usually can't be done in your domain DNS — it requires control of the IP address block.

Understanding Your Health Score

80-100 (Excellent): ✓ All checks pass — your domain is well-configured
50-79 (Fair): ⚠ Some warnings — improve to avoid spam folder
0-49 (Poor): ✗ Critical issues — emails likely going to spam

💡 Pro tip: Even one "fail" can tank your deliverability. Fix all red items first, then address warnings. After making DNS changes, wait 24-48 hours for propagation before re-testing.

🎯 What To Do Next

  1. Click "Audit DNS" to see your current score
  2. Copy the exact TXT record values from the "Fix if missing" sections above
  3. Log into your domain registrar (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Cloudflare, etc.)
  4. Add the TXT records in your DNS settings
  5. Wait 24-48 hours for DNS propagation
  6. Run the audit again to confirm your score improved

Need help? Most domain registrars have guides for adding DNS records. Search "[your registrar] add TXT record" for step-by-step instructions.
